How every brief is made, checked and scored

Innovation Daily briefs one buildable AI-driven business per episode. This page says exactly what stands behind each one - and what does not.

Not advice Innovation Daily is research and commentary. It is not financial, legal, tax or investment advice, and nothing here is a recommendation to spend your money. Capital figures are estimates, ratings are editorial judgments, and markets move after a brief is published. Talk to a qualified professional before you commit money, and do your own checking - the validation kit on each episode exists for exactly that.

Where ideas come from

Every episode draws from a curated pool of business concepts. Each entry names who pays, why the need exists now, the real competitors, an execution complexity rating, a capital plan under one hundred thousand dollars, and a ninety-day start plan. The model that writes the brief never invents the business, the competitors or the money - it writes around those fields.

An idea can only be picked once it is verified. Ideas that make a legal or regulatory claim a listener could act on are held until the owner signs off on the wording. New ideas start as a one-line seed; Claude researches the seed with live web search and drafts a full entry with its sources, which stays in review until the owner approves it. If the research cannot find real competitors or sources, the seed is marked insufficient rather than filled in with guesses.

What "verified" means

Checked, with the date shown

  • Every named competitor was checked live: it exists, it does roughly what the brief says, and its owner and name are current.
  • Every number, superlative or named-source claim in the entry links to a source that supports it - or it was removed.
  • Each episode shows its sources and the date they were last checked.

Not verified

  • Capital figures. They are planning estimates, not quotes.
  • The complexity, AI leverage and defensibility ratings. They are editorial judgments.
  • Anything that changed after the check date.

How a brief is written and checked

  1. The writing model receives the entry, a note on what each competitor actually is, and a list pairing each verified source with the one claim it supports. It may only credit a claim to a source that the list pairs with it.
  2. A second, stronger model fact-checks the draft against exactly the same material, sentence by sentence: numbers not in the material, credits to a source that does not say it, overstated claims, unsupported superlatives, invented causes or history, misdescribed competitors, and personal financial advice.
  3. Flagged sentences get one rewrite. Anything the check still flags after that is cut before the audio is made - a missing sentence is better than a false one.
  4. The audio is generated, and its published length is measured from the file itself, not estimated.

Episodes published before September 29, 2026 were not fact-checked this way. Where a claim in one of them failed the later verification pass, the episode carries a correction note.

The Innovation Score (version 2)

A directional 0-100 read to make the archive comparable - not a valuation and not a prediction. It is computed in code from the entry and its verification record; the model never assigns it, so the same idea always scores the same.

WeightComponentHow it is computed
30%DefensibilityThe editorial moat rating, 1 to 5, scaled to 0-100.
20%Market validation100 if at least one named company is already paid for this outcome by this buyer; 60 if named companies sell only something adjacent; 20 if none can be named. One paying competitor is proof enough - more do not add points.
15%Room to enterStarts at 100. Minus 25 for each venture-funded or public company selling the same thing to the same buyer; minus 10 for each other named direct competitor. Floor of 0. A crowded market costs points here; it never earns them.
15%AI leverageThe editorial rating, 1 to 5, of how much of the work is genuinely model-native.
10%Execution easeThe inverse of the 1-to-5 complexity rating.
10%Capital efficiency100 x (1 - estimated capital / 100,000)
Strong 72+ Solid 58-71 Speculative 45-57 Weak under 45

Moat caps. A defensibility rating of 1 cannot score above Speculative, and a rating of 2 cannot score above Solid - the number is held at the top of that band. A Strong idea needs a real moat.

What changed from version 1. Version 1 scored market validation as more points for more competitors, so a crowded market looked better, and gave capital efficiency a quarter of the weight, which rewarded cheap, easily copied services. Version 2 separates proof of demand from crowding and puts the most weight on the moat. Archive scores were recomputed under version 2 on September 27, 2026.
